# Operating Model

## 1. Decision priority

Use this order when priorities conflict:

1. Patient safety
2. Legal, compliance, and fixed-deadline obligations
3. Patient value and continuity of care
4. Staff capability and reduction of single-person dependence
5. Productivity, queue stability, and KPI performance
6. Research, education, and reusable knowledge

A short-term metric does not override a higher-priority safety, compliance, or patient-value concern.

## 2. Role model

| Role | Primary responsibility |
|---|---|
| Site lead | Whole-system optimization, resource allocation, final escalation, portfolio review |
| Domain owner | Outcome for one domain, weekly review, corrective action |
| Shift/operations lead | Real-time flow decisions and escalation during operation |
| Experienced staff | Delegated routine decisions within explicit boundaries |
| Independent reviewer | Required check for designated high-risk work |
| Learner/frontline member | Execute standard work, report exceptions, maintain one development action |

Names and internal titles are intentionally excluded.

## 3. Review cadence

### Daily

- Identify the highest safety or continuity concern.
- Check the live bottleneck.
- Reallocate only when an observable trigger is met.
- Record one item that requires later management action.

### Weekly

- Review P0 first.
- Review KPI rates and denominators.
- Compare weekday/time-band demand and capacity.
- Confirm staff feedback and delegation progress.
- Close completed or obsolete work.
- Add new work only if it outranks existing work.

### Monthly

- Review role capacity and management time.
- Check whether controls reduced risk.
- Review patient-value, productivity, and capability together.
- Decide whether research or AI projects should proceed, pause, or change scope.

## 4. Data definitions

### Service wait time

From customer reception to customer call. This represents the experienced queue interval, subject to the limits of the local waiting-time measurement process.

### Internal processing time

From the start of order/prescription entry to completion of the post-service administrative step. It spans internal work and may continue beyond the customer's waiting experience.

### On-site capacity

Attendance time minus breaks, off-site assignments, and other unavailable intervals. Overlapping activity records are not additional employees or additional paid capacity.

### Main comparison set

Use ordinary weekdays for the primary operational analysis. Analyze weekends and holidays separately when their hours, demand, or staffing patterns differ.

## 5. Improvement method

1. Define the problem with the correct measure.
2. Identify the constrained step and time band.
3. Choose one controllable change.
4. State the expected mechanism.
5. Test for a limited period.
6. Review outcome and unintended effects.
7. Adopt, modify, or stop.
8. Record only the decision and reusable lesson.

## 6. Task hygiene

- One task equals one observable action.
- Every task has a completion condition.
- Use role-level ownership in this public repository.
- Do not copy incomplete tasks into multiple files.
- Do not let new suggestions hide unfinished P0/P1 work.
- Close, re-scope, or explicitly defer stale tasks.
- Keep confidential evidence outside this repository.

## 7. Research and AI guardrails

- Define the question and outcome before selecting a model or tool.
- Distinguish association, reporting signal, prediction, and causation.
- Address selection, confounding, missingness, measurement error, clustering, and generalizability.
- Use minimum necessary data and approved environments.
- Require human review for clinical interpretation.
- Evaluate patient value and unintended burden, not only time saved.
